#### Why learn to code when AI can do everything?
1. You can still code recreationally. People still play chess for fun even though the best chess bots beat 99.99% of chess players.
2. You still need to know how stuff works if you want to be a software engineer.
3. Gain an appreciation for the technologies that shape our world.

#### Why me?
Most advice found online about how to learn to code essentially boils down to this:
1. Uh...just follow a tutorial or read the documentation or something to learn the syntax
2. Then just do a couple projects and you'll learn everything along the way, thats what worked for me no reason why it can't work for you!

This is absolutely horrible advice for beginners. This was the advice given to me when I was first starting. There's just so many questions a beginner does not know how to answer. Such as:

- Which programming language to start with?
- Do I need to memorize anything? How will I remember syntax?
- What tutorial do I follow? How do I read documentation?
- What should I do for my projects?
- How do I solve problems?
- How do I design my projects?

... etc etc

which leads to people (myself included) trying a bunch of YouTube tutorials, getting overwhelmed and giving up.

I find that most people who give this advice are either:
1. People who studied a university degree that involved some coding or took a coding bootcamp, where people are extremely motivated to learn and you have essentially infinite free time to learn. You are probably not this person.
2. People who learnt coding from a book in the 70s and got a job by simply asking for one, then learning other technologies along the way all while getting paid to do so. You are also probably not this person either.
3. Savants who started coding when they were 6 and got their masters degree before most people graduate high school. You are definitely not this person.

I'm not saying these people did not work hard, but the average person learning to code is probably not any of the above so their advice is probably not the most reasonable. 

Luckily for you dear reader, like you, I am none of the above. 

I studied chemistry in university, not a single line of code written. I am not from the 70s nor am I a savant. I taught myself, after multiple failed previous attempts, how to code at night whilst doing sales internship. And now I am a full time software engineer!

#### Why WDFZ?
I believe the [Full Stack Open](https://fullstackopen.com/en) course is still the best way to learn to code. It's what I used and what I recommended to everyone. The content is clear and easy to follow. They give exercises and projects without handholding and without vague requirements. And best of all, not only is it 100% free, they even give you a certificate when you are finished! 

However, if you are beginner with absolutely zero coding experience/knowledge you probably wont get very far. WDFZ is here to bridge that gap. I hope to teach you everything you need to start the course as well the meta skills like debugging, reading documentation, searching which are never taught to beginners.

But more importantly. I want to give you a "I know Kung Fu moment"

<YouTube id="https://youtu.be/0YhJxJZOWBw" />

Initially knowing nothing about fighting, Neo has martial arts uploaded into his mind over several hours. Culminating in the infamous 'I know Kung Fu' line. And proceeds to be able to spar with Morpheus. 

Learning to code is the same, you start from absolutely nothing. Then as you learn to code, you eventually reach a similar moment, when you realize you are able to create anything you want with your barehands. And from that point any additional learning is trvial. 

Most beginners never reach this point simply due to receiving poor instruction, and as a result, they give up before reaching this point.
